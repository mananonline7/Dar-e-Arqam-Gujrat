Across the Region

Days That Shape a Well-Rounded Student

Academics fill most of the calendar, but not all of it. Across our campuses, students also mark the days that build awareness, empathy and pride — for their culture, their country, their planet and the people around them. Each one below is celebrated a little differently from campus to campus, but the purpose stays the same.

Cultural Day

Cultural Day

Once a year, our campuses set aside the uniform for something older — the dress, crafts and customs of the regions our students come from. Children arrive in traditional attire, join in mehndi and folk activities, and sit among handmade pottery and props that turn a school courtyard into a small mela for the day. It's a simple, joyful reminder that the culture they've inherited is worth knowing and wearing with pride.

Iqbal Day · 9th November

Iqbal Day

Every 9th of November, students pause to remember Allama Muhammad Iqbal — the poet-philosopher whose words shaped how a nation thinks about itself. Younger children dress as characters from his poems for young readers, while older students prepare posters and short speeches on his message to the youth: "The destiny of nations is in the hands of individuals; every individual is a star in the fate of the nation." It's less a history lesson than an invitation to read his words and take them seriously.

Color Days

Color Days

For our youngest learners, a single colour takes over the day — blue, yellow, red, brown and more, each with its own theme, its own decorations and its own dress code. Beyond the fun of matching outfits, Color Days build vocabulary, observation and confidence in the classroom's smallest students, one shade at a time.

Defence Day · 6th September

Defence Day

On the 6th of September, students honour the soldiers who defended Pakistan in 1965 and the servicemen who continue that duty today. Memorial walls carry the names and photographs of the fallen, young cadets salute in uniform, and older students perform short tributes recalling what was given so the rest of the country could carry on. It's one of the more solemn days on the calendar, and deliberately so.

Earth Day

Earth Day

Earth Day turns the classroom's attention outward — to recycling, planting, pollution and the small habits that add up. Students build model dioramas of a cleaner planet, design posters urging their schoolmates to "treat every day like Earth Day," and take part in activities meant to make environmental responsibility feel personal rather than abstract.

Labour Day · 1st May

Labour Day

On the 1st of May, students step into the shoes of the people whose work usually goes unnoticed — the sweeper, the gardener, the barber, the doctor. Through simple role-play and classroom activities, Labour Day teaches a quiet but important lesson: that every kind of work has dignity, and every worker deserves respect.

Teachers Day

Teachers Day

Teachers Day flips the usual direction of gratitude — for one morning, it's the students' turn to say thank you. Petals are scattered along the path teachers walk to assembly, handmade cards are exchanged, and the whole school gathers to applaud the people who show up for them every single day. It's simple, and that's exactly the point.

Rabi-ul-Awwal · Ten Days of Seerah
Ashra Seerat-un-Nabi ﷺ

عشرہ سیرت النبی ﷺ

Ashra Seerat-un-Nabi ﷺ

رکھ کر نبی ﷺ کو سامنے، آرائشِ کردار

“Keeping the Prophet ﷺ before us — the adornment of character.”

Every year, as the month of Rabi-ul-Awwal arrives, every Dar-e-Arqam campus sets aside ten days for one purpose: to place the life of the Holy Prophet ﷺ before our children, and to let it shape them.

Dar-e-Arqam arranges Ashra Seerat-un-Nabi ﷺ across all campuses to cultivate in students a deep love and devotion for the Holy Prophet Khatam-un-Nabiyyeen ﷺ, and to build a real awareness of his blessed life. Every aspect of the Seerah of the Prophet ﷺ is a source of guidance and blessing for us.

To introduce today's generation to that Seerah — and to bring a lasting, positive change into their lives — the programme was arranged under the theme رکھ کر نبی ﷺ کو سامنے، آرائشِ کردار: keep the Prophet ﷺ before you, and let your character be adorned by him. It is a theme deliberately aimed not at information, but at conduct.

دارِ ارقم ہر سال ربیع الاول کے مہینے میں طلبہ میں حضور خاتم النّبیّینﷺ سے محبت اور عقیدت پیدا کرنے اور آپ خاتم النّبیّینﷺ کی زندگی سے آگاہی پیدا کرنے کے لیے تمام کیمپسز میں عشرہ سیرت النبی ﷺ کا اہتمام کرتا ہے۔ سیرت رسولﷺ کا ہر پہلو ہمارے لیے باعث رہنمائی اور برکت ہے۔

Through those ten days the Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ and the Hadith were committed to memory by the children on a daily basis. Events from his blessed life were brought alive through qissa khwani — storytelling — and through the study of the book Muhammad ﷺ Arabi. The children took part with genuine eagerness and delight, and shared their own reflections in the form of videos.

جس میں سنت رسولﷺ اور حدیث کو روزانہ کی بنیاد پر بچوں کو ازبر کروایا گیا، اس کے ساتھ نبی ﷺ کی زندگی سے متعلق واقعات کو قصہ خوانی اور محمد ﷺ عربی کتاب کا مطالعہ کروا کر کیا گیا۔ جس میں بچوں نے ذوق و شوق سے حصہ لیا اور اپنے تاثرات کا اظہار ویڈیوز کی صورت میں شئیر کیا۔

Independence Day

Pakistan Independence Day Celebration

“Green for the majority, white for the minorities — one flag, one nation.”

Dar-e-Arqam Schools, Gujrat Region

Every 14th of August, our campuses turn green and white overnight — and for one morning, every student in the Gujrat Region stands a little taller, sings a little louder, and remembers exactly what the day cost to earn.

Independence Day at Dar-e-Arqam is never just a holiday marked on the calendar. Corridors are dressed in bunting the week before, classrooms compete to build the proudest wall display, and by the morning of the 14th every courtyard is ready for an assembly that belongs as much to the students as it does to the country. The flag is raised at sunrise by our own cadets, the national anthem is sung standing and in full voice, and for a few minutes the whole campus is silent except for one shared song.

What follows is a morning built entirely by our students — speeches on freedom and sacrifice, tableaus that bring history back onto a school stage, patriotic performances rehearsed for weeks, and the kind of unity that only shows itself when hundreds of children choose, together, to wear the same two colours. Younger students learn the names of Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah and Allama Iqbal before they can fully understand what those names mean; older students perform the speeches that explain it to them. Between the two, an entire school learns to love a country it is still growing up to inherit.

One School, One Flag Fatima Campus — every student became a single thread in the same flag.

Celebrations at a Glance

Every tradition that makes the morning of the 14th feel the way it does.

Flag Hoisting Ceremony

Our own cadets raise the flag at sunrise while the whole campus stands to attention — the moment every celebration begins from.

National Anthem & Songs

Sung standing, in full voice, followed by milli naghmay that turn the whole assembly into one chorus.

Speeches on Freedom

Students take the microphone to speak on sacrifice, freedom and what independence still asks of their generation.

Patriotic Tableaus

Costumed performances — soldiers, regional dress, moments from the Pakistan Movement — brought back onto a school stage.

Balloon & Flag Release

Green and white balloons let go together, sent up under the portraits of Quaid-e-Azam and Allama Iqbal.

Campus Decoration

Corridors, stages and classroom walls dressed green and white for a week — every campus competing for the proudest display.

That is what an Independence Day morning at Dar-e-Arqam is really for.
